# A1 – [Build Your Professional Portfolio]
Portfolio Analysis 

Website 1 - Luke DeVries

Navigability: This portfolio is organized so the reader can locate any assignment without searching through unrelated pages. The "Analysis of an Everyday Object" heading clearly marks where the stapler analysis begins and where his student introduction ends. Within his analysis, multiple photographs and descriptions make it easy to locate the information about the stapler's components. 

Reproducibility: This portfolio provided a large amount of information about how the stapler is constructed and how the stapler functions. This student included photos of the magazine, clinching anvil, body, and other features, and then explained how each part operates. The student then discusses possible manufacturing processes, such as metal forming and injection molding. However, I don't believe another engineer could reproduce the stapler from this document because it doesn't provide exact dimensions, material specifications, tolerances, or manufacturing drawings, which are crucial for reproducing the product.

Evidence of Reasoning: Yes, I believe this portfolio provides evidence of how engineering decisions were made rather than only presenting the final result. For example, the student explains how the geometry of the stapler's clinching anvil guides and bends the staple legs. He also explains why the anvil is designed to remain attached to the stapler rather than being separated. The student also connects the shapes and materials of the different components to the possible manufacturing process. These explanations show the reasoning behind the stapler's design decisions.

Professional Tone: The portfolio uses engineering terminology and supports the analysis with original photos, patent drawings, a patent number, and descriptions of each component. This makes the portfolio appropriate for an engineering audience. However, shorter sentences, more headings, and more specific measurements would make the portfolio easier for an employer or engineer to understand and read.

Website 2 - Nathaniel Karau

Navigability: The portfolio is really easy to navigate because the main menu separates the website into clearly labeled sections such as Home, About Me, Skills, Work Experience, Projects, and Contact. This makes it easy for any reader to quickly find and move to a specific section without searching through the entire portfolio. In the project section, each project is neatly organized with its own title, picture, and a "Click to View" button that lets the reader view the project's contents. 

Reproducibility: The portfolio does not provide enough information for another engineer to reproduce the projects without asking for additional information. The student describes only the project and work experience, including CAD, FEA, machining, assembly, and testing. He does not provide any information that would allow another engineer to reproduce the projects that he has done, such as dimensions, calculations, and engineering drawings. As a result, the portfolio focuses mainly on the final product rather than the decision-making process that produced these results.

Evidence of Reasoning: The portfolio does show some signs of engineering reasoning because the student explains the decisions and results of several of his engineering decisions. For example, the student discusses using CAD and FEA modeling to check structural integrity. The student also covers the outcome of each project, such as "maintaining ±1° angular accuracy and reduced manual effort for single-user operation." This was the outcome of his tilt table.

Professional Tone: This portfolio does use engineering terminology such as CAD modeling, FEA, design validation, machining, prototyping, and design for manufacturability. This portfolio also covers the student's work experiences and accomplishments using specific results. In my opinion, this makes the language appropriate for any employer reviewing the student's engineering portfolio.
